People considering nutrition blood testing in Tokyo may want to understand whether tiredness, dizziness, changes in energy, or other symptoms could be related to their nutritional status.

A nutrition blood test can provide information about selected nutrients and related health markers. However, one test cannot explain every symptom or confirm that a nutrient deficiency is the only cause. Results should be reviewed together with symptoms, medical history, medications, supplements, and other test findings.

## What Is Included in Nutrition Blood Testing?

The exact tests depend on the clinic and the purpose of the assessment.

Pitonne’s [Nutrition Blood Testing service](https://pitonne.jp/services/blood-tests/) adds a nutrition evaluation to standard and hormone blood testing. The panel may include:

- Iron
- Ferritin
- Vitamin B12
- Folate
- Vitamin D
- Zinc
- Other standard health markers
- Thyroid and sex hormone markers, when appropriate

Not every person needs every test. The appropriate panel should be considered based on the person’s symptoms, health information, and reason for testing.

## What Can Iron and Ferritin Testing Show?

Iron tests can measure different markers related to iron levels and iron storage.

A [ferritin blood test](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/ferritin-blood-test/) measures the amount of ferritin in the blood. Ferritin is a protein that stores iron in the body.

Depending on the situation, testing may also include:

- Serum iron
- Transferrin
- Total iron-binding capacity
- A complete blood count
- Other tests related to anemia

Iron testing may be considered when someone has tiredness, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, heavy menstrual bleeding, or a history that may increase the risk of low iron.

However, these symptoms have many possible causes. A low iron result does not automatically explain every symptom, and it may be necessary to investigate why the level is low.

## What Can Vitamin B12 and Folate Testing Show?

Vitamin B12 and folate are involved in the formation of healthy blood cells and the normal function of the nervous system.

The [NHS explains that vitamin B12 or folate deficiency may be assessed using symptoms and blood tests](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamin-b12-or-folate-deficiency-anaemia/diagnosis/). Testing may include:

- Vitamin B12
- Folate
- Hemoglobin
- Red blood cell size
- Other tests, when needed

People may discuss B12 or folate testing if they have ongoing tiredness, weakness, numbness, tingling, mouth changes, memory concerns, or a restrictive diet.

Some people may have symptoms with results that appear within the reference range. Others may have low levels without obvious symptoms. The results therefore need to be interpreted with the person’s overall health information.

## What Can Vitamin D and Zinc Testing Show?

Vitamin D and zinc testing may provide additional information about nutritional status.

The results may be useful when considered alongside:

- Diet and eating patterns
- Limited sun exposure
- Digestive conditions
- Previous surgery
- Long-term medication use
- Current supplements
- Ongoing health conditions

A blood level does not always show the full picture of nutrition. It also does not prove that a vitamin or mineral is responsible for a particular symptom.

Low energy, changes in mood, hair changes, or frequent illness may have nutritional, hormonal, lifestyle-related, or medical causes. Additional review may be needed before deciding what treatment is appropriate.

## Who May Consider Nutrition Blood Testing?

Nutrition blood testing may be worth discussing when someone has:

- Ongoing tiredness or low energy
- Dizziness or weakness
- Heavy menstrual periods
- A restrictive or limited diet
- A vegetarian or vegan diet
- A history of digestive problems
- Previous surgery affecting digestion
- Unexplained changes in appetite or weight
- Long-term use of certain medications
- A known nutritional deficiency
- A need to review supplement use

This does not mean that everyone with these concerns needs a nutrition blood test. A nurse or physician can help decide whether testing is appropriate.

## What Can Nutrition Blood Testing Not Confirm?

A nutrition blood test can provide useful information, but it cannot answer every health question.

The results alone cannot always:

- Explain all causes of tiredness
- Confirm that a symptom is caused by one nutrient
- Show the quality of a person’s entire diet
- Diagnose every digestive or hormonal condition
- Determine the correct supplement dose
- Prove that IV therapy is necessary
- Replace a medical consultation
- Confirm that a person is generally healthy

A result outside the reference range does not always mean that treatment is required. A result within the reference range also does not necessarily explain every symptom.

## What Should You Tell the Clinic Before Testing?

Before nutrition blood testing, tell the clinic about:

- Current symptoms
- When the symptoms started
- Your usual diet
- Vegetarian, vegan, or restrictive eating patterns
- Heavy menstrual bleeding
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding
- Prescription medications
- Over-the-counter medicines
- Vitamins and supplements
- Previous blood test results
- Digestive conditions or surgery
- Recent IV therapy or other treatments

Ask whether you need to fast and whether the timing of any supplements matters. Do not stop prescribed medication or supplements without professional advice.

It may also be helpful to write down the main question you want the test to investigate. For example, you may want to discuss persistent tiredness, a previous low ferritin result, or whether your current supplements are appropriate.

## What Happens After the Test?

Once the results are available, a nurse or physician should review them with you.

Depending on the results, the next step may include:

- No further testing
- Repeating a test
- Additional blood tests
- Reviewing medications or supplements
- Dietary advice
- Oral supplementation
- A consultation with another healthcare professional
- Investigating another possible cause of the symptoms

If a deficiency is suspected, it is also important to consider why it occurred. Possible causes may include diet, blood loss, reduced absorption, medication, or another health condition.

A nutrition blood test should support a broader health assessment rather than replace it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does a nutrition blood test check?

Depending on the panel, it may check iron, ferritin, vitamin B12, folate, vitamin D, zinc, and other health markers. The exact tests should be selected based on the person’s symptoms and health information.

### Can a nutrition blood test explain tiredness?

It may identify a nutritional issue that requires further review, but tiredness can also be related to sleep, stress, anemia, thyroid problems, infection, medications, or other conditions.

### Should I stop taking supplements before testing?

Do not stop supplements on your own. Tell the clinic exactly what you take and ask whether any supplement should be paused before testing.

### Can the test tell me exactly what I should eat?

The results may provide useful information, but they do not create a complete diet plan by themselves. Dietary advice should consider the results, health history, allergies, lifestyle, and personal goals.

## Conclusion

Nutrition blood testing in Tokyo can provide information about iron, ferritin, vitamin B12, folate, vitamin D, zinc, and other health markers.

The results may be useful when someone has ongoing tiredness, dizziness, a restrictive diet, heavy menstrual periods, digestive concerns, or a known nutritional issue. However, the results should not be interpreted alone or used to decide on supplements or IV therapy without professional advice.

If you are considering nutrition blood testing at the Pitonne clinic or as part of a mobile appointment, discuss your symptoms, diet, medications, supplements, and main concerns with the nurse or physician before testing.
