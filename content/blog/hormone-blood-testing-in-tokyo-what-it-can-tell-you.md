People considering hormone blood testing in Tokyo may want to understand whether fatigue, weight changes, irregular periods, menopause symptoms, or low testosterone concerns could be related to hormone levels.

Hormone blood testing can provide useful information about the levels of selected hormones at the time of testing. However, one result does not always explain every symptom or confirm a diagnosis by itself. Results should be interpreted together with symptoms, medical history, medications, age, and other test findings.

## What Is Included in Hormone Blood Testing?

The exact tests depend on the clinic and the purpose of the assessment.

Pitonne’s hormone blood testing service includes standard health markers together with thyroid hormone and sex hormone panels. It is designed for people who are concerned about fatigue, weight changes, irregular periods, menopause symptoms, or low testosterone. [Pitonne’s blood testing service](https://pitonne.jp/services/blood-tests/) provides further information about the available panels.

Possible hormone-related tests may include:

- Thyroid-stimulating hormone, or TSH
- Thyroxine, or T4
- Triiodothyronine, or T3
- Estrogen or estradiol
- Testosterone
- Follicle-stimulating hormone, or FSH
- Luteinizing hormone, or LH
- Other hormones depending on the person’s symptoms

Not every person needs every hormone test. The appropriate panel should be decided based on the reason for testing and the person’s health information.

## What Can Thyroid Hormone Testing Show?

Thyroid hormone testing can help assess how the thyroid is functioning.

The [NHS explains that thyroid function tests may measure TSH, T3, and T4](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/overactive-thyroid-hyperthyroidism/diagnosis/). These results may help healthcare professionals investigate symptoms that could be related to an overactive or underactive thyroid.

Symptoms that may lead someone to discuss thyroid testing include:

- Ongoing tiredness
- Unexplained weight changes
- Feeling unusually hot or cold
- Changes in heart rate
- Difficulty sleeping
- Changes in bowel habits
- Changes in mood or concentration

These symptoms can also have many other causes. A hormone blood test does not automatically show that the thyroid is responsible.

## What Can Sex Hormone Testing Show?

Sex hormone testing may help assess hormone levels in situations involving menstrual changes, menopause-related concerns, fertility-related questions, or testosterone concerns.

Depending on the person, testing may include estrogen, testosterone, FSH, or LH. For example, [MedlinePlus explains that estrogen testing may be used to investigate conditions associated with estrogen levels that are too high or too low](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/estrogen-levels-test/).

Possible reasons to discuss sex hormone testing include:

- Irregular periods
- Changes associated with menopause
- Reduced libido
- Changes in energy or mood
- Concerns about testosterone levels
- Difficulty interpreting symptoms that may have more than one possible cause

Hormone levels can change according to age, sex, menstrual cycle, pregnancy status, medication, and the timing of the test. A result should therefore be interpreted in context.

## What Can Hormone Blood Testing Not Confirm?

Hormone testing can provide information, but it does not answer every health question.

A single result may not be enough to:

- Explain all causes of fatigue
- Confirm menopause in every situation
- Diagnose a condition without considering symptoms
- Determine treatment without additional review
- Show that hormone treatment is necessary
- Prove that a symptom is caused by one hormone

Test results may also vary depending on the laboratory’s reference range and the timing of the blood draw.

For example, the NHS notes that symptoms commonly associated with low testosterone can also be related to stress, depression, anxiety, lifestyle, or other physical conditions. A testosterone result should therefore be considered alongside the person’s symptoms and overall health.

## What Should You Tell the Clinic Before Testing?

Before hormone blood testing, provide accurate information about:

- Current symptoms
- When the symptoms started
- Menstrual cycle information, if relevant
- Menopause status
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding
- Prescription medications
- Hormonal contraception or hormone replacement therapy
- Vitamins and supplements
- Previous hormone tests
- Relevant medical conditions

Some medicines and hormone treatments can affect test results. Do not stop medication or hormone treatment before testing unless a healthcare professional instructs you to do so.

It may also be useful to write down the main question you want the test to help investigate. This can make the consultation more focused.

## What Happens After the Test?

Once the results are available, a healthcare professional should review them with you.

The next step may be:

- No further action
- Repeating a test
- Ordering additional blood tests
- Reviewing medication
- Arranging a consultation with a specialist
- Investigating another possible cause of the symptoms

A result outside the reference range does not always mean that treatment is required. Similarly, a result within the reference range does not necessarily explain every symptom.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does a hormone blood test check?

A hormone blood test measures selected hormones in a blood sample. Depending on the purpose of testing, it may include thyroid hormones, estrogen, testosterone, FSH, LH, or other markers. The exact tests should be selected according to the person’s symptoms and health history.

### Can hormone blood testing explain fatigue?

It may help identify whether certain thyroid or sex hormone levels require further review, but fatigue has many possible causes. Sleep, stress, anemia, nutrition, infection, medications, and other health conditions may also contribute to tiredness.

### Can a hormone test confirm menopause?

Not always. Menopause is often assessed using age, menstrual history, and symptoms, and hormone levels can fluctuate. The meaning of a test result depends on the individual situation and should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

### Should I stop medication before hormone testing?

No. Do not stop, reduce, or change prescribed medication or hormone treatment without medical advice. Tell the clinic what you are taking so the results can be interpreted correctly.

## Conclusion

Hormone blood testing in Tokyo can help provide information about thyroid and sex hormone levels when someone is experiencing fatigue, weight changes, irregular periods, menopause-related symptoms, or concerns about testosterone.

However, hormone blood testing is not a complete diagnosis by itself. Results should be reviewed together with symptoms, age, medical history, medications, and other relevant tests.

If you are considering hormone blood testing at the Pitonne clinic or as part of a mobile appointment, discuss your main concerns and current health information with the healthcare professional before testing.
