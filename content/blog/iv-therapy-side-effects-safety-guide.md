## What side effects can occur with IV therapy?

IV therapy involves delivering fluids, vitamins, medication, or other substances directly into a vein. The possible reactions depend on the ingredients, dosage, infusion speed, method of administration, and the individual’s health condition.

Some people may experience temporary discomfort at the insertion site, such as mild pain, redness, bruising, or swelling. Other symptoms, including dizziness, nausea, palpitations, or a general feeling of discomfort, may also occur depending on the contents of the infusion.

Not every person experiences these reactions, and the same IV treatment may affect people differently. Before treatment, ask a qualified healthcare professional about the ingredients, possible reactions, expected duration, and alternatives.

## What to tell your healthcare provider before treatment

Before receiving IV therapy, share accurate information about your health and medical history. This may include current symptoms or health concerns, previous medical conditions, medications and supplements, known allergies, previous reactions to injections or infusions, recent treatments or procedures, and changes in your condition since booking.

If you have a fever, breathing difficulty, chest pain, repeated vomiting, severe dizziness, or a significant change in your condition, contact a medical professional before receiving IV therapy.

IV therapy is not appropriate for every health concern. When symptoms may indicate an urgent medical condition, emergency evaluation should take priority over mobile IV therapy.

## Symptoms to report during an infusion

Tell the nurse immediately if you experience strong pain, burning, swelling, or unusual discomfort around the IV insertion site.

In some cases, fluid or medication can move into the tissue surrounding the vein instead of remaining inside the vein. Continuing the infusion without assessment may place additional stress on the surrounding tissue. Do not wait for the symptoms to disappear on their own.

You should also report dizziness, nausea, palpitations, shortness of breath, throat discomfort, rash, or a sudden feeling of weakness during treatment.

## What to check after IV therapy

After the infusion, observe both the insertion site and your overall condition.

Contact your healthcare provider if redness, swelling, pain, bleeding, or bruising becomes worse, or if you develop a fever. MedlinePlus lists redness, swelling, bruising, pain, bleeding, and a temperature of 38°C or higher as symptoms that should be reported to a healthcare provider in the context of home IV treatment.

[Source: MedlinePlus — IV treatment at home](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000496.htm)

The appropriate aftercare may vary depending on the infusion ingredients and your condition. If you have an important meeting, flight, long-distance journey, or event after treatment, allow extra time in case you need to rest or contact the clinic.

## When to seek urgent medical help

Seek urgent medical attention if you develop difficulty breathing, chest pain, severe dizziness or fainting, a very fast heartbeat, swelling of the face, lips, or throat, widespread hives or rash, confusion, or a sudden change in consciousness.

MedlinePlus advises seeking emergency help for breathing problems, a fast heart rate, dizziness, or chest pain after IV treatment.

Do not assume that severe symptoms are simply a normal response to an infusion. If symptoms are sudden, severe, or rapidly worsening, contact local emergency services.

## Safety considerations for mobile IV therapy

Mobile IV therapy may take place at a home, hotel, or office. Before the visit, confirm the location, access instructions, room number, contact details, treatment contents, estimated duration, and what to do if your condition changes.

Mobile IV therapy is not a substitute for emergency care. If you have severe symptoms or a possible emergency, seek urgent medical attention first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are IV therapy side effects guaranteed?

No. Possible reactions vary depending on the ingredients, dosage, health condition, allergies, and other individual factors.

### What should I do if the IV site hurts?

Tell the nurse immediately if you experience strong pain, swelling, burning, or unusual discomfort. Do not continue the infusion without professional assessment.

### Is bruising after an IV normal?

Minor bruising may occur after needle insertion. However, increasing redness, swelling, pain, continued bleeding, pus, or fever should be reported to a healthcare provider.

### Can IV therapy cure fatigue or other symptoms?

IV therapy is not a guaranteed solution for fatigue or other health concerns. The suitability and expected effects depend on the individual’s condition and the treatment contents. Discuss possible benefits, risks, and alternatives with a healthcare professional.

## Conclusion

IV therapy may cause local reactions such as pain, redness, swelling, or bruising at the insertion site. Depending on the contents and the individual’s condition, other symptoms may also occur.

If you notice pain or swelling during the infusion, tell the nurse immediately. After treatment, contact a healthcare provider if symptoms continue, worsen, or are accompanied by fever.

Breathing difficulty, chest pain, severe dizziness, fainting, or changes in consciousness require urgent medical attention rather than waiting for a mobile IV visit.
